## Why it matters

An overall ANOVA can show that treatment means are not all equal but does not say which pairs differ. Fisher’s least significant difference turns the experiment’s common error estimate into a threshold for pairwise comparisons.

## Intuition

Each difference of means has a standard error derived from replication and the pooled residual variation. A pair is called different when its observed gap exceeds a $t$ critical value times that standard error.

## Prerequisites

Assume a randomized experiment with a valid common error mean square $MSE$, residual degrees of freedom $\nu$, and treatment means based on independent groups. The simplest formula below allows unequal replication.

## Formal statement

For treatment means $\bar Y_i$ and $\bar Y_j$, the two-sided threshold at level $\alpha$ is

$$LSD_{ij}=t_{1-\alpha/2,\nu}
\sqrt{MSE\left(\frac1{n_i}+\frac1{n_j}\right)}.$$

The pair is declared different when $|\bar Y_i-\bar Y_j|>LSD_{ij}$. A “protected” procedure first requires a significant overall ANOVA F test.

## Worked example

If $MSE=9$, $n_i=n_j=10$, $\nu=36$, and $t_{0.975,36}\approx2.03$, then $LSD\approx2.03\sqrt{9(0.2)}\approx2.72$. Means differing by $3$ pass the pairwise threshold.

## History and attribution

Fisher developed the method in the same experimental-design program that established ANOVA and pooled error estimation. It was intended for planned scientific comparisons, not as a license to search every pair after seeing the data. Later work formalized familywise error and showed when the preliminary F test offers protection.

## Modern use

LSD comparisons remain common in agricultural and biological experiments, especially with a small number of preplanned contrasts. The Fisher–Hayter procedure is Hayter’s later modification using a studentized-range critical value; it is not the original LSD. Model-based estimated marginal means generalize the calculation to more complex designs.

## Limits and debates

With many unplanned pairwise comparisons, ordinary LSD does not control the familywise error rate. A significant omnibus test does not generally cure this problem for more than three groups. Tukey, Dunnett, Holm, or prespecified contrasts are often more appropriate, depending on the scientific question.
