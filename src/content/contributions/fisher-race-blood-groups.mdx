## Why it matters

Rh incompatibility can cause transfusion reactions and haemolytic disease of the fetus and newborn. A workable genetic notation helped laboratories communicate serological findings and predict inherited antigen combinations before the molecular locus structure was known.

## Intuition

Antibody reactions suggested several paired factors—C/c, D/d, and E/e—that tended to travel together through families. Fisher and Race represented each chromosome by a haplotype such as $CDe$ or $cde$, making observed blood types consequences of paired haplotypes.

## Prerequisites

Serology detects antigen phenotypes rather than DNA sequence directly. A haplotype records variants inherited together on one chromosome; phase may be ambiguous when only antigen reactions are observed.

## Formal statement

In the historical Fisher–Race notation, an individual inherits one three-symbol haplotype from each parent. For example, $CDe/cde$ predicts the presence of C, c, D, and e antigens but not E. Modern molecular genetics instead locates the principal antigen system in the linked **RHD** and **RHCE** genes.

## Worked example

A parent with haplotypes $CDe/cde$ produces $CDe$ or $cde$ gametes with probability one half under the simple no-recombination account. Crossed with $cde/cde$, the model predicts half $CDe/cde$ and half $cde/cde$ offspring.

## History and attribution

Fisher and Race jointly proposed the triplet account and published gene-frequency work in 1946. Competing Wiener notation reflected a different genetic interpretation; later molecular evidence superseded both models’ literal locus claims while parts of their antigen notation survived. The work was conducted within British blood-group genetics and the Galton Laboratory’s eugenic institutional setting. Fisher’s scientific contribution does not erase his sustained advocacy of eugenics, nor should that advocacy be used to obscure Race’s co-authorship and the clinical importance of the research.

## Modern use

Transfusion medicine still uses C, c, D, E, and e antigen labels, while genotyping resolves variants and haplotypes that serology cannot. Historical notation remains necessary for reading clinical and genetic literature.

## Limits and debates

The three-locus theory is not a correct molecular map: **RHD** and **RHCE** plus many variants produce the system. The missing “d” antigen is a notation for absence of D, not a detected antigen. Historical population categories and racialized sampling must be interpreted critically rather than treated as natural biological divisions.
