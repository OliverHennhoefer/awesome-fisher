## Why it matters

The ordinary hypergeometric distribution assumes equal selection odds. Fisher’s noncentral version introduces an odds parameter while retaining fixed margins, making it the alternative distribution naturally paired with conditional tests of a $2\times2$ table.

## Intuition

Imagine two groups producing successes independently but with different odds. After conditioning on the total number of successes, the only random quantity is how many landed in the first group. Values favoring that group receive exponentially greater weight when its odds are higher.

## Prerequisites

Let group sizes be $m_1,m_2$, let $X$ be the first group’s success count, and condition on $X+Y=n$. Write $\omega$ for the ratio of success odds in the two groups.

## Formal statement

On feasible integers $x$, the probability mass is

$$P(X=x\mid X+Y=n)=
\frac{{m_1\choose x}{m_2\choose n-x}\omega^x}
{\sum_u {m_1\choose u}{m_2\choose n-u}\omega^u}.$$

At $\omega=1$ this reduces to the central hypergeometric distribution used by Fisher’s exact test.

## Worked example

With $m_1=m_2=2$, $n=2$, and $\omega=2$, the unnormalized weights for $x=0,1,2$ are $1,8,4$. After division by $13$, the probabilities are $1/13,8/13,4/13$.

## History and attribution

Fisher used this conditional model in *The Design of Experiments*. The explicit eponym and its consistent separation from Wallenius’s distribution came later. Wallenius’s noncentral hypergeometric law describes sequential biased sampling without replacement; it is not merely another parameterization of Fisher’s conditional-binomial law.

## Modern use

The distribution supports exact confidence intervals for odds ratios, biased sampling models, contingency-table power calculations, conditional logistic methods, and multivariate count extensions.

## Limits and debates

The two noncentral hypergeometric families are frequently confused in software and prose. Their data-generating mechanisms and probabilities differ. Computation can also be delicate for large counts or extreme odds, requiring stable recurrence or specialized sampling algorithms.
