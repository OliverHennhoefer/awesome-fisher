## Why it matters

Ranks are robust to monotone changes of measurement but have uneven spacing. Normal scores map them to a symmetric, interpretable scale that approximates where ordered observations from a standard normal sample would fall.

## Intuition

The smallest observation receives a negative score, the median a score near zero, and the largest a positive score. Extreme ranks are farther apart because normal tails are farther apart on the quantile scale.

## Prerequisites

For a sample of size $n$, let $R_i$ be observation $i$’s rank. Exact Fisher–Yates scores are expectations $\mathbb E[Z_{(r)}]$ of standard-normal order statistics; common formulas approximate them with normal quantiles.

## Formal statement

A widely used approximation is

$$z_i=\Phi^{-1}\left(\frac{R_i-a}{n+1-2a}\right),$$

where $\Phi^{-1}$ is the standard-normal quantile and $a$ is a plotting-position constant. Exact expected order-statistic scores are not identical to every such rank inverse-normal formula.

## Worked example

With $n=5$ and the simple $a=0$ rule, ranks $1$ through $5$ map to $\Phi^{-1}(1/6),\ldots,\Phi^{-1}(5/6)$, approximately $[-0.967,-0.431,0,0.431,0.967]$.

## History and attribution

Fisher and Yates published tables of normal scores in 1938 for practical rank procedures. Their joint eponym refers specifically to expected normal order statistics. Later plotting-position constants, inverse-normal transformations, and normal-score tests should not be attributed wholesale to them.

## Modern use

Normal scores appear in rank tests, robust correlation, quantitative genetics, nonparametric combination methods, and preprocessing of skewed variables. They preserve order and can reduce the leverage of raw outliers.

## Limits and debates

The transformation changes units and marginal shape but does not make a multivariate data set jointly normal or repair confounding. Results depend slightly on the plotting-position convention and treatment of ties. Applying scores before model validation can conceal scientifically meaningful tail structure.
