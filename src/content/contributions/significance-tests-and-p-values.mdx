## Why it matters

P-values became one of science’s most common—and most commonly misread—summaries. Understanding Fisher’s own evidential interpretation helps separate it from later fixed-threshold decision rules.

## Intuition

Assume a concrete null model and ask how far into the model’s tail the observed result lies. A small p-value says the data are unusual under that null and chosen test statistic. It does not say the null has a small probability of being true.

## Prerequisites

You need a null model, a statistic selected before seeing the result, and its distribution under the null. “More extreme” must be defined in relation to the scientific question.

## Formal statement

For statistic $T$ where larger values count as more discrepant, an upper-tail p-value is

$$p=\Pr_{H_0}\{T(X)\geq T(x_{obs})\}.$$

When the null distribution is continuous and correctly specified, p-values are uniformly distributed under $H_0$.

## Worked example

If a fair coin is tossed 20 times and produces 18 or more heads, a one-sided binomial p-value sums the null probabilities for 18, 19, and 20 heads. The calculation assesses the fair-coin model in that direction; it does not supply the posterior probability that the coin is fair.

## History and attribution

Fisher promoted tail probabilities as graded evidence and often used 0.05 as a convenient reference, not a universal law. Neyman and Egon Pearson developed a distinct framework of repeated-decision error rates. Present-day “NHST” often fuses the vocabularies while respecting neither framework cleanly.

## Modern use

Significance tests remain useful when the null, statistic, sampling plan, and interpretation are transparent. They are strongest alongside effect estimates, uncertainty intervals, diagnostics, and domain knowledge.

## Limits and debates

A p-value is not an effect size, replication probability, false-positive probability, or measure of practical importance. Selection, optional stopping, multiple testing, weak models, and publication bias can all destroy its nominal interpretation.
