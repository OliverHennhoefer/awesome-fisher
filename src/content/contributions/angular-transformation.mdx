## Why it matters

A raw binomial proportion has variance $p(1-p)/n$, so its uncertainty changes with its mean. Fisher’s transformation was an early way to put proportions on a scale with approximately constant variance before generalized linear models were readily available.

## Intuition

The square root spreads values near zero, while the arcsine bends the scale near both zero and one. Together they expand the boundaries and compress the middle just enough to offset the binomial mean–variance relationship to first order.

## Prerequisites

For $X\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,p)$, write $\hat p=X/n$. Conventions differ by a factor of two: some authors use $\arcsin\sqrt{\hat p}$ and others use twice that angle.

## Formal statement

With $g(p)=2\arcsin\sqrt p$, the delta method gives

$$g'(p)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{p(1-p)}}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\operatorname{Var}\{g(\hat p)\}\approx\frac1n.$$

Thus the transformed variance is approximately independent of $p$ away from difficult boundary cases. Anscombe-type corrections adjust $X$ and $n$ to improve small-sample behavior.

## Worked example

For $40$ successes in $100$ trials, $g(0.4)=2\arcsin\sqrt{0.4}\approx1.369$ radians. The approximate standard error on this convention is $1/\sqrt{100}=0.1$. Interpretation must return to the proportion scale by $p=\sin^2(g/2)$.

## History and attribution

Fisher used the angular transformation in his 1922 work on the dominance ratio. Later corrections and applications form a broader lineage; not every arcsine-based procedure is a separate Fisher contribution. The transformation became especially common in biology and ecology.

## Modern use

It remains useful for historical replication, some variance-stabilizing calculations, and specialized meta-analytic settings. Its simple standard-error approximation can also illuminate why raw proportions have unequal precision.

## Limits and debates

For ordinary binomial regression, a model with a binomial likelihood and interpretable link is usually preferable. Transformation can discard denominator information, complicate interpretation, behave awkwardly at boundaries, and still fail under overdispersion. Modern critiques therefore warn against applying it by reflex.
