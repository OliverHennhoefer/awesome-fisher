## Why it matters

One newborn, one breeding adult, and one post-reproductive adult do not contribute equally to a population’s future. Reproductive value supplies the weights needed to aggregate heterogeneous classes without counting every individual as demographically equivalent.

## Intuition

A class has high reproductive value when its members are likely to survive and produce many descendants, especially soon. Contributions far in the future are discounted by population growth because a growing future population makes any one descendant a smaller share of the whole.

## Prerequisites

For a population projection matrix $L$, the class-abundance vector changes by $n_{t+1}=Ln_t$. Let the dominant eigenvalue $\lambda$ be the long-run growth factor.

## Formal statement

The reproductive-value vector $v$ is the positive left eigenvector

$$v^\top L=\lambda v^\top.$$

Then total reproductive value grows coherently: $v^\top n_{t+1}=\lambda v^\top n_t$. Its overall scale is arbitrary, so it is usually normalized relative to a chosen class or to the stable class distribution.

## Worked example

For $L=\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\0.5&0\end{pmatrix}$, the dominant eigenvalue is $1$. A left eigenvector satisfies $v_2=2v_1$, so a member of the second class carries twice the reproductive value of a member of the first under this model.

## History and attribution

Fisher introduced reproductive value in *The Genetical Theory of Natural Selection* while connecting demography and selection. Later work in matrix population models and evolutionary theory gave the concept its modern eigenvector form and linked it more rigorously to gene-frequency change.

## Modern use

Reproductive-value weighting appears in age-structured selection, kin selection, conservation demography, epidemiology, and multitype branching processes. It helps distinguish raw head counts from contributions relevant to long-run ancestry.

## Limits and debates

Values depend on the demographic model, environment, time scale, and class definition. Density dependence and changing environments can prevent a fixed eigenvector from describing the future. Reproductive value is a mathematical weight, not a moral judgment about the worth of organisms or people.
